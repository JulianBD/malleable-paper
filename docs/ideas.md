# Ideas — breadth pass, 19 September 2026

Four ideation agents read the pitch, the walkthrough, the README and the
source, each with one lens, and were told that everything is buildable and
implementation is not a concern. Their reports are reproduced below with the
agents' own groupings and their ★ picks. Nothing here is a decision.

Lenses: **A** prior art and adjacent worlds · **B** the UI itself ·
**C** the data model and the agents · **D** no constraints, ten to fifty years out.

## A · Prior art and adjacent worlds (77)

### Hypertext, Bush, Engelbart
1. ★ Xanadu transclusion: a phrase pinned on another frame is a live transclusion; edit the source and every copy updates, origin thread visible.
2. Bush's trails: a named sequence of tends across frames; replaying it re-walks the reasoning, one tend at a time.
3. Two-way links: every generated heading lists back-links to other frames where your phrases were grouped under the same label.
4. Engelbart view-specs: keystroke prefixes (`:1`, `:aq`) filter the structure to one nesting level or one type, log untouched.
5. NLS statement addresses: each authored span gets a stable address (3B2) citable from other frames and the command line.
6. Nelson's stretchtext: hold a key on a lifted phrase to expand its surrounding sentence in place, glue in blue.

### Smalltalk, Boxer, HyperCard, Sketchpad
7. ★ Boxer boxes: a group is a box you open; inside is the same paper with its own text, tends and policies.
8. HyperCard background layer: frames are cards; a stack's background holds standing policies and pinned phrases shared by every card.
9. Smalltalk inspector: the ⋯ menu becomes a live object browser with claims, basis events, producer, and "senders" links.
10. Sketchpad constraints: pin two phrases and declare "keep these adjacent"; the scanner treats the constraint as durable evidence.
11. HyperTalk scripts as policy: "when a phrase says call, mark it action" is written in plain English, shown in the policy line.
12. doesNotUnderstand: an unparsed command becomes a proposed policy claim the paper asks about, never a silent failure.

### Dynamicland, Bret Victor, Muse
13. Realtalk claims: every producer output is literally a wish/claim triple in the drawer, matchable by other producers.
14. Show the data: hover a confidence number to see the sentences from your past frames that scored it.
15. Muse ink margin: scribble a mark beside a block; the scanner reads the mark's shape as a tend.
16. Dynamicland proximity: two paper index cards placed near each other under a camera emit `near` implications on the plane.
17. Ladder of abstraction: slide from "this entry" to "all entries this month"; structure regenerates at that scope, phrases unchanged.

### Roam, Obsidian, Tana, Notion
18. Tana supertags: a confirmed type promotes the phrase to a schema-bearing object whose empty fields become missing-data intents.
19. Obsidian canvas: the plane accepts whole frames as cards; dragging a frame near a phrase proposes a cross-frame connection.
20. ★ Roam daily notes: arrow-right from today is tomorrow; unresolved actions carry forward as transcluded phrases, with a faint origin date.
21. Notion database views: a confirmed "many objects of one kind" implication summons a table whose columns are their shared claims.
22. Block reference counts: a phrase shows how many frames transclude it; click to walk them.
23. Graph view: zoomed out, frames are dots and confirmed connections lines; nothing generated appears at this altitude.

### Spreadsheets and Excel's implicit models
24. ★ Implicit model: a repeated measure across frames ("slept 6h") summons an x-y plot with your phrases as data labels.
25. Formulas as claims: the paper proposes "this number depends on those two"; confirming draws a dependency arrow.
26. Fill-down: confirm a type on one phrase, then drag its marker down to apply the same tend to siblings.
27. Goal seek: name a target in your words ("sleep 7h"); the paper highlights which authored actions the log correlates with it.
28. Trace precedents: for any claim, arrows run back to the exact events and phrases it rests on.

### Zettelkasten, commonplace books, marginalia
29. Luhmann branching ids: a nested phrase gets 12a3; ids are anchors that survive regrouping and rewriting.
30. Commonplace lexicon: the paper offers headings only from a tended lexicon built from your own past phrases.
31. Marginalia: type beside a block; the margin note is authored text and becomes basis for a claim.
32. Manicule: place a pointing-hand mark beside a phrase; it means "return to this" and becomes a remember intent.
33. Folgezettel: a phrase continuing another proposes a `follows` claim, drawn as a thread between them.

### Bullet journals, GTD, quantified self
34. Migration: at frame end, unresolved actions are offered carry / schedule / drop; the arrow glyph is the bullet journal's.
35. GTD contexts: a confirmed action proposes a context (@phone, @home) drawn only from words in the same entry.
36. Rapid-logging signifiers: typing `•`, `○`, `–` before a phrase pre-tends its type; markers double as shorthand.
37. Repeat counter: a phrase you keep writing ("coffee again") summons a one-tap counter log, schema generated on demand.
38. Weekly review frame: a summoned frame lists the week's confirmed and deferred claims as your phrases, no summary.

### Zen, Stoic, CBT journaling
39. ★ CBT thought record: a vent intent above threshold summons situation / thought / evidence-for / evidence-against slots filled only by your spans.
40. Stoic evening review: three fixed questions as generated labels; answers are new authored text placed beneath them.
41. Morning pages: a "no structure until I say" policy; the paper reads silently and shows only a count of what it would lift.
42. Distortion labelling: the paper proposes "all-or-nothing?" as a tentative type; a no is durable, it never asks again.
43. Koan frame: a reflection with no resolution is offered a scratch frame with the phrase alone, centred.
44. Gratitude strand: phrases read as gratitude accumulate on one pinned strand across frames you can walk.

### Talmud, music, typography, cartography
45. ★ Talmudic page: the authored paragraph sits centre; the implication list and older tends wrap it as collapsible commentary layers.
46. Dynamics: confidence rendered like pp–ff in marker weight; a hairpin shows evidence growing across frames.
47. Rehearsal letters: recurring themes get A, B, A' so a week's entries show their form at a glance.
48. Legend: one corner legend explains marker geometry and colour, replacing repeated inline generated labels.
49. Contour lines: density of confirmed claims draws faint contours on the plane; where you think most is higher ground.
50. Tufte sidenotes: the reason for each claim sits as a numbered sidenote aligned to its block.
51. Rubrication: all generated language in one rubric colour, as scribes marked what was not the text.

### Lab notebooks, medical charts, flight decks, ATC
52. Lab-notebook rule: nothing erased; a rewrite strikes through the old phrase and the strike is itself an event.
53. SOAP note: subjective / objective / assessment / plan slots that accept only your spans, summoned by a decide intent.
54. ★ ATC strips: each open action is a strip in a rack; you slide it between pending, active, done bays by hand.
55. Challenge-response checklist: each sub-step is posed as a question; a keypress answers, the answer is an event.
56. Trend flag: a value drifting across frames (mood, sleep) raises a small margin flag, pure geometry, no words.
57. Sterile cockpit: a policy silencing all proposals while writing; they arrive only after you press done.

### Law, journalism, detective boards, playbooks
58. Case file: a decision gathers evidence phrases under for / against tabs; the verdict is your own final sentence.
59. String board: pinned phrases across frames joined by red thread; "why" on a thread shows the claim basis.
60. Chain of custody: every tend shows who (human or which producer) touched the claim and when.
61. Playbook arrows: an action with sub-steps summons X-and-O style arrows ordering the phrases.
62. Sourcing tiers: claims marked single-source, corroborated, or confirmed by you, using the same three ring glyphs.

### Game UIs and interactive fiction
63. ★ Thought cabinet: a deferred reflection internalises over days; when it resolves, the paper resurfaces one of your related phrases.
64. Her Story: type a word; every frame containing it surfaces as a clip in your words, never summarised.
65. Parser IF: an unrecognised command replies with what the paper could act on, as a list of your phrases.
66. Tarot spread: three random past reflections laid in past / present / future slots; the juxtaposition is the prompt.
67. Skill check: a proposal shows its odds as a die face; a rejected one stays visible as "the paper was unsure".
68. Fog of war: unvisited frames on the plane are dimmed; visiting reveals them and connections into them.

### Local-first, CRDT, event sourcing, Datalog, Semantic Web
69. Datalog policies: each policy is a readable rule; the drawer shows which rule fired for which claim.
70. ★ Time-travel scrubber: drag along the event log; the structure reforms at any past moment, provenance animated backwards.
71. ★ CRDT multiplayer: two people on one frame; each person's tends a separate colour, disagreements drawn as a fork, never merged.
72. Triples export: `subject|claim` is already RDF; export a frame as Turtle so other tools read the implications.
73. Producer diff: replay the log through the regex reader and a model reader; show the two implication lists side by side.
74. Compensating events: undo appends a reversal rather than deleting, so the drawer shows changed minds.

### Architecture drawing, sports, other
75. Revision clouds: when a producer changes an inference, a faint cloud outlines what moved, as architects mark revisions.
76. Section cut: choose an axis (time, topic) and the plane shows a section through frames along it.
77. Bill of quantities: counts of actions, questions, reflections per week, built from your phrases only, no adjectives.

### Tensions (A)
1. Stoic review (40), CBT slots (39), SOAP (53) inject fixed generated prompts. Contract wins: prompts are questions, summoned only above threshold, each a held-back-able agent intention.
2. Talmudic commentary (45), sidenotes (50), inspector (9) risk crowding the authored centre. Authored wins: layers collapsed by default, revealed on hold or hover, never at rest.
3. Thought cabinet (63), GTD contexts (35), goal seek (27) tempt the paper to originate content. Contract wins: it may only resurface, reorder, or correlate existing phrases; a gap is a missing-data intent, not new words.
4. Multiplayer tends (71) and Dynamicland cards (16) make "whose tend is authoritative" ambiguous. Tendability wins over consensus: tends keyed by author, both persist, the fork is shown, no auto-merge.
5. Sterile cockpit (57) and morning pages (41) hide inferences the reader still makes. "Every inference is tendable" wins: silence is a doer policy only; the reader keeps running and the list stays inspectable.

## B · The UI itself (108)

### New targets
1. ★ The gap: hover the whitespace between two sections; a hairline rule appears; click to ask "why apart?"; drag to merge.
2. The marker itself as a target: click cycles type, hold fans the six shapes out in an arc around it.
3. The rule under a heading is a handle: drag it down to swallow the next block into the group.
4. The glue: in raw mode, click a faded connective to promote it to an authored object ("this matters").
5. The connection line: hover the dotted teal line to see the near-claim's confidence; drag its midpoint to add a third object.
6. The margin between column and plane is a target: dropping there means "pin, but keep flow order".
7. The title-row rule: drag it right to widen the column, drag left to split into two columns.
8. The paper's own state word ("reading", "structured") is clickable: it opens the loop diagram with the current stage lit.
9. A confidence: click the number in the tend menu to set a threshold ("show me nothing below this").
10. The policy line at the bottom: each token is a target; click to cycle its value, no typing.

### New inputs and gestures
11. ★ Scrub: drag horizontally on blank space to scrub between the paragraph and the structure; release snaps to nearer end.
12. Pinch on trackpad: zoom out to see the frame as one of nine on the plane; pinch in returns.
13. Shake a block (fast small drag) to shed all derived claims on it: it drops back to "as written".
14. Double-click a word inside a span to split the object at that word, the split animates apart.
15. Press-and-hold a proposal button: confidence needed rises while you hold; release below threshold cancels.
16. Two-finger tap on a block on touch opens the tend menu; one-finger long-press lifts it.
17. Draw a lasso with Shift-drag: the selection becomes a group target with its own tend menu.
18. Type a letter while hovering blank space to filter the frame to that marker type; Esc clears.
19. Drag a block onto a margin arrow to move it to the adjacent frame; the arrow swallows it.
20. Keyboard-only tending: j/k walk blocks, y/n/l answer the focused proposal, the focused marker pulses.
21. Drag one object onto another's marker (not text) to say "same kind as this".

### Motion and provenance
22. ★ Every generated heading is born from its member spans: it emerges as a thin line rising from them, then hardens into letters.
23. Rejected claims do not vanish; the block's marker cracks (two halves slide apart) and fades over 800ms.
24. When a policy changes, the affected blocks breathe once (2px outward scale) before relaying, so you see what the policy touched.
25. The FLIP motion's easing shows the reader's certainty: confident spans move straight, tentative spans overshoot and settle.
26. Hover a heading: its member spans' source positions in the paragraph glow amber for as long as you hover.
27. Deferred claims sink: the block drops 4px and lightens; a later re-emission lifts it back with a soft bounce.
28. Provenance trail: after a relayout, a 2px amber thread lingers 1s along each span's path, then dissolves.
29. Superseded implications play backward: the old marker unrotates into the new one rather than cross-fading.

### Confidence, shown
30. ★ Marker fill is confidence: 0.4 a thin outline, 0.7 half-filled like a moon phase, 1.0 solid; green once confirmed.
31. Letter-spacing of a generated label widens as confidence falls; "SLEEP" tight, "S L E E P" loose.
32. Tentative blocks sit 2px left of the column's true left edge; confirmed blocks sit flush.
33. The rule under a section is dashed at proportion to how many members are tentative.
34. A claim's basis is drawn as a hairline from the marker to the word in the span that triggered it, on hover.
35. Blocks below the confidence threshold render in the replica only: visible when you hold Space, invisible otherwise.

### Typography and colour
36. Authored text in a serif again, but only at 18px; below 16px everything is sans, so scale carries class.
37. Colour on the ground, not the ink: a tentative section has a 6px pastel bleed at its left edge only.
38. Generated labels never use letters longer than the span they came from; if longer, they abbreviate to initials.
39. A frame's colour temperature drifts with age: today pure white, a week-old frame #fdfcf8.
40. Small caps for authored headings, uppercase sans for generated ones: two kinds of "heading" at a glance.

### New proposal shapes
41. ★ Slider proposal: for a degree claim ("how badly?") a 2px line with an amber knob; dragging is a tend.
42. Order proposal: two blocks show a faint ⇅ between them; click to swap, the swap is the claim.
43. Absence proposal: a dashed empty bullet at the end of a group says "nothing else here?"; typing fills it, clicking dismisses.
44. Measure proposal: "same word three days running" offers a tiny x-y plot as a 60px inline sparkline.
45. Rename proposal: hover a generated heading and the paper offers one authored phrase from the group instead.
46. Split-column proposal: "these two topics never touch"; accepting draws a vertical 2px rule and two columns.
47. Timebox proposal: an action grows a small ring that fills over the day; click to snooze, it drains.
48. Counter-proposal: after "no", the toggle offers the second-most-likely type rather than asking again.
49. Question proposal in the raw: a ring marker appears in the paragraph margin next to a sentence that reads as a question.

### New ways to tend
50. Tend by dragging the marker: pull a square off a block and drop it on another to move the action claim.
51. Tend by rewriting: edit the span in place; the reader re-runs on the new text but keeps every tend on the old key.
52. Tend by silence: a proposal ignored for three visits fades to 30% and stops asking; it's a deferred tend.
53. Batch tend: Shift-click three "yes" buttons; a single 2px rule connects them; one click confirms all.
54. Tend the tender: the ⋯ menu's last row is "why did you ask?" and opens the policy that produced the claim.
55. Undo as a tend: Cmd-Z after accepting a claim records a rejection, never a silent revert.

### Plane and flow
56. ★ Pinned blocks cast a faint slot in the column where they came from; drag back to the slot returns them.
57. The plane has a grid only when you drag: 8px dots appear under the cursor within a 200px radius.
58. A pinned cluster with three or more members gets a generated heading of its own, born from its members.
59. Distance is meaning: two pins within 60px are "near", 60–200px "related", beyond is nothing; rings show the bands while dragging.
60. Drag the whole column: the flow itself is pinnable; a frame can have two columns and a scatter.
61. Flow-to-plane spring: drop a block on the plane and it drifts 20px toward its nearest semantic neighbour before settling.

### What a frame is adjacent to
62. ★ Right is tomorrow, left is yesterday, up is the week, down is the detail: the arrows carry the axis names on hover.
63. The up-frame is a digest frame: only confirmed blocks from the four below, laid out by the same reader.
64. Diagonal frames are scratch: no reader, no doer, plain paper; drag a block there to think without proposals.
65. An arrow shows a 1px sliver of the adjacent frame's first line on hover, like the edge of a page.
66. Frames can be adjacent by claim: a "reference" block's triangle points to a frame; click follows it.
67. A frame adjacent to a person: the frame to the right of "text Sam back" is the Sam frame.

### Raw, beyond the paragraph
68. ★ Raw in time: hold Space and scroll to watch the paragraph being typed, deletions included, at 10x.
69. Raw for the reader: hold Shift-Space to see the regexes or model spans that fired, drawn as underlines.
70. Raw for the plane: holding Space over the pinned layer shows the drop coordinates and the near-bands, not the paragraph.
71. Raw for a heading: hold Space over a generated label to see the three candidate labels it beat.
72. Raw for a policy: hold Space over the policy line to see the utterance that set it and the diff it caused.

### Writing back in time
73. On return after a day, the frame opens on the replica and the structure relays live: the paper re-reads in front of you.
74. Weekly frame: the same phrase across five frames ("slept badly") lifts into one row; the FLIP crosses frame boundaries.
75. An action older than a week loses its square: it becomes a ring ("still?"); answer or it becomes a reflection.
76. The paper writes one generated word per day at most, in the margin, aligned to the block it's about.
77. The soundboard reorders itself by what you actually did (typed "texted Sam"), not by what it proposed.
78. Streak as geometry: a confirmed intention across N days draws N stacked 2px rules under it, no numbers.

### Sound and haptics
79. Lift is a paper rustle; settle is silence; a proposal arriving is one soft tick; a rejection is a shorter tick.
80. Haptic on the trackpad when a dragged block crosses a near-band; a second heavier tick when it enters "nest".
81. Silence as signal: the paper never sounds during typing; the first sound after a pause means "reading".

### Ambient state
82. The brand dot's pulse rate is the idle timer: it slows as the two seconds run, stops at the read.
83. The top bar's state word is the only status: never a spinner, never a toast, only a word that changes.
84. A frame you have not tended shows a single 2px amber rule at the very bottom edge of the viewport.
85. The favicon is the marker of the most tentative claim on the current frame.

### On a phone
86. One column, no plane; pinning is a long-press that lifts the block into a bottom shelf; shelf order is nearness.
87. Swipe a block right to confirm the top proposal, left to reject, the marker slides with it and fills.
88. The command line is the keyboard's suggestion strip: seeds appear where autocorrect would.
89. Margin arrows become edge swipes; the adjacent frame peels in like a page.
90. Space-hold becomes thumb-hold on the title: the paragraph shows through while your thumb is down.

### On a wall, in AR
91. On a wall the plane is real scale: pins are pinned where you stand; the frame grid is the room's walls.
92. In AR the four adjacent frames hang in the four directions; you turn your head to read yesterday.
93. Proposals on a wall arrive at eye height only; nothing above or below asks anything.
94. Two people at one wall: each person's tends are the same green, but the marker rotates 45° toward the tender.

### On paper, with a pen
95. Print the frame: markers print as their shapes, tentative ones as dotted outlines you can fill in with a pen.
96. Pen on tablet: circling a phrase pins it; a stroke between two pins is a connect tend; a strike is a reject.
97. Pen pressure sets confidence: press hard on a marker to confirm, light to defer.
98. Handwritten paragraph is the raw; the lifted spans are typeset, the glue stays in your hand.

### Voice
99. The command line listens only while "/" is held; the transcript appears as monospace violet, never as paper.
100. Speaking while a block is focused tends it: "no", "later", "that's a question" map to tends, nothing else.

### Multi-device
101. The phone is the raw: it holds the paragraph; the desk shows the structure; hold the phone to the screen and it aligns.
102. A second screen is the adjacent frame to the right, permanently; drag across the bezel to move a block into tomorrow.
103. The watch is the soundboard: it shows one next move at a time; tapping confirms, waiting defers.

### Things without names
104. Weight: a block that has been tended many times sits with 1px more line-height below it, as if heavier.
105. Ghost of a rejected claim: hover the block and a pale outline of the marker it was refused appears, once.
106. The paper's hesitation: before a low-confidence proposal appears, its slot holds empty for 400ms, then fills.
107. Refusal to structure: when the paper cannot read a paragraph it draws nothing at all and lightens the rule under the title.
108. The margin sentence: once a week the paper writes back one authored phrase of yours in the margin, no label, no verb.

### Keep it spartan (B)
- Sound (79–81), haptics (80) and the AR/wall pieces (91–94) add channels the aesthetic never had; allow them only as one-bit events tied to existing state changes, never as ornament, off by default.
- Grid dots (57), near-band rings (59) and provenance threads (28) are chrome; they exist only during a drag and die with pointer-up.
- Sparklines (44), timebox rings (47) and streak rules (78) are widgets; draw them with the marker's own 2px stroke and pastel, no axes, no numbers.
- Colour-temperature drift (39), bleeds (37) and ghosts (105) touch the white ground; keep them under 3% delta and only on hover or age.
- Phone shelves (86), swipe reveals (87) and bezel drags (102) borrow app idioms; keep them geometric, sans, labelled only in 12px uppercase, and make the marker the thing that moves.

## C · The data model and the agents (101)

### New claim kinds
1. `same-as`: two phrases across frames name one thing; confirming merges their tend histories, never their text.
2. `measure`: a phrase carries value, unit, time ("slept 5h"); confirmed measures feed the x-y plot log.
3. `when`: a phrase implies a date or recurrence; basis is the connective plus the frame's visit timestamp.
4. `about-person`: the clause concerns a named other; gates sharing and raises the privacy cost of derived claims.
5. `contradicts`: two confirmed claims cannot both hold; shown as a question on the newer, never auto-resolved.
6. `stale`: a confirmed action unrevisited for N frame visits; a claim about time, tendable like any other.
7. `answered-by`: a later clause resolves an earlier question; confirming links frames and retires the soundboard move.
8. `needs-data`: the reader lacks evidence; confirming becomes an agent intention to create a log or ask.
9. `presentation`: "this set wants a table"; a widget summons is a claim whose basis is the object set.
10. `segment`: splits and merges become claims (`obj|split=12`), so segmentation is tendable and supersedable like types.
11. `belongs-to-frame`: a clause plausibly belongs in an adjacent frame; confirming moves it with its tends intact.
12. `mood`: valence on a clause from lexicon plus biometrics; rendered only as marker colour, never as a word.

### New producers
13. ★ Every producer is a pure function of (log slice, policies) declaring its inputs; undeclared inputs are unreadable, so provenance is total.
14. Calendar: events become reference objects with `when` claims; overlap with authored "tomorrow" raises confidence, never rewrites.
15. Location: a place log emits `near=place` claims; nearness is evidence, same rule as the scanner's 48px.
16. Biometrics: HRV and sleep emit `measure` claims into a body log; consented once per log, revocable by tend.
17. Messages: a quoted message is a reference object in the sender's words, marked authored-by-other, never paraphrased.
18. Web: a pasted URL yields a reference plus `answered-by` candidates; fetched text stays outside the frame, cited by hash.
19. Past self: replays old frames as a reader emitting `same-as` and `stale`; confidence decays with frame age.
20. Other papers: a shared frame's confirmed claims arrive here as proposed, basis pointing into their log.
21. Silence: no writing about a confirmed intention is data; emits `stale` with basis "no frame since".
22. Widgets: a checklist tick emits typed `status=done` events into its log; the reader treats them as tends.
23. Dictation: per-clause timestamps give the reader pause length as segmentation evidence.
24. Correction patterns: reads the human's own tends across frames and emits policy claims: "you reject action on 'I should' 80%".

### Decay, merge, compete, budget
25. ★ Confidence decays per frame visit, not per second; a claim untouched across N visits withdraws itself; tends never decay.
26. Same key from different producers merges by noisy-OR of confidence, bases concatenated, reasons kept separately.
27. Competing claims on one subject and kind form a slate; only the winner is proposed, losers superseded, all visible.
28. A per-frame proposal budget in attention units; each shape has a price; the doer packs by confidence times price.
29. A rejection propagates to siblings: rejecting `type=action` lowers `intent=resolve` on the same subject.
30. Supersession is explicit: the new claim's basis names the old key, so the drawer shows lineage, not disappearance.
31. Confidence is never shown as a number; only motion duration, tentativeness and question form carry it.
32. A claim below threshold for three visits moves to a cold list: replayable, never rendered.
33. The list is bounded by reader recall, not storage: a producer must reproduce a claim each run or it lapses.

### Multiple agents on one list
34. ★ Each agent has a named intention set in policy; `producer` becomes an agent id; heldBack is per agent.
35. Agents never edit each other's claims; they emit counter-claims on the same key with a different basis; the slate resolves.
36. A quiet agent and a bold agent share one reader but hold different budgets; one command tends their split.
37. Agent disagreement is itself a claim (`key|contested`), shown as a toggle pair, the widget for one ambiguity.
38. An agent's intention set is a frame of its own; its claims about what it wants are tendable in the same UI.
39. Negotiation is a fold: agents append bids to the log; a deterministic auction reducer picks proposals; no agent chat.
40. A specialist agent (health, money) attaches only to logs it declares; the human sees which logs feed which agent.

### Agents that build
41. ★ Missing evidence emits a schema claim: the agent proposes a log with typed fields; confirming creates log and widget.
42. A summoned widget declares semantics (field, unit, event kind) so every drag lands as a typed event, never a blob.
43. Channels are logs with an external writer; the agent proposes "ask Sam", the human confirms, replies become events.
44. Schemas are versioned claims; migrating a log is a superseding schema claim with a pure upgrade function in its basis.
45. A widget never tended after N visits withdraws its schema claim; its log stays, replayable, invisible.
46. An agent proposes a producer ("read your calendar"); confirming is the consent event and the producer's root basis.

### Queries as objects
47. ★ A query is a keyed object with body, result log and tends; the human sees it as a block in the frame.
48. A query's result is a producer's input; the drawer shows query, row count, and which claims rest on it.
49. Queries can be authored by dragging: two frames near on the plane propose a cross-frame query with `near` semantics.
50. A query is idempotent over the log; results are never stored, only their hashes, for provenance.
51. Query cost shows as rows touched; expensive queries are proposed, cheap ones run unasked.

### Provenance and basis chains
52. ★ Every basis entry resolves to an event id, object id, query hash or claim key; nothing else is admitted.
53. Hovering a claim walks the chain back to authored pixels; if it crosses into a foreign log, the hover names the owner.
54. A tend records the basis it saw; if that basis later changes, the tend is flagged "tended on old evidence", never silently kept.
55. Generated labels carry a basis: the section label "Sleep" names the keywords and claims that produced it.
56. A claim resting only on other claims is derived; the reader caps chain depth by budget.

### Time, forks, counterfactuals
57. ★ A fork is a log prefix plus a divergent suffix; "what if I had said no" replays the prefix with one tend swapped, purely.
58. The plane gains a time axis: arrow up is the same frame's earlier version, derived by cutting the log at an index.
59. Versions are cut points, not copies: an event index plus a hash of the reduced state.
60. Counterfactual runs are instrumentation events, never authoritative, but their proposals can be tended into the real log.
61. A confirmed `when` claim schedules a future frame visit that the calendar producer will fill.
62. Replaying with a newer reader shows a diff of implication lists; the human tends the diff, not the new list.

### Privacy and forgetting
63. ★ Forgetting is a tombstone event on a key or range; the reducer treats it as absent; snapshots are re-derived, never edited.
64. Text is stored as content-addressed chunks; forgetting deletes the chunk, the log keeps the hash, replay yields a gap.
65. Claims about a person carry a privacy class; sharing drops them unless that person consented in their own log.
66. Producers declare a data class; biometric claims never cross a frame boundary without a sharing tend.
67. A claim whose basis is tombstoned withdraws with reason "evidence forgotten".
68. "What does the paper know about X" lists claims filtered by subject, each with a tombstone affordance.

### Sharing a frame
69. A shared frame is two logs merged in deterministic order; every event carries its author; tends are per author, never merged.
70. The other person's agent sees my confirmed claims as proposed, my authorship in basis; symmetric for them.
71. Conflicting tends on one key by two people yield a `contested` claim, shown to both as a toggle pair.
72. Sharing scopes are queries: "share what's under Sleep" shares that result log, live, revocable by tombstone.
73. Another person's authored text renders in their weight, never mine; the three UI classes become four.

### Economics
74. Each proposal costs attention; each tend pays back; the doer's per-frame budget is the running balance.
75. A tend is worth more when the claim was uncertain: confirming 0.5 teaches more than confirming 0.9.
76. Producers spend from a budget set in policy; a costly model producer runs only when cheap ones are unsure.
77. Ignored proposals are negative signal: unseen through three visits, they debit the intention that raised them.
78. Cross-frame queries cost rows; the agent shows the cost as a claim before running.

### Trust and calibration
79. ★ Every tend is a labelled example; calibration per producer and claim kind is recomputed on replay, from the log alone.
80. A producer whose 0.7 claims are rejected half the time gets an isotonic remap before proposal.
81. Trust is per intention: a miscalibrated intention is held back automatically and shown as a policy claim.
82. Calibration shows as motion: well-calibrated claims fly faster; the human learns the feel without numbers.
83. The human can tend a producer: "you're too sure about actions" patches its curve as a policy event.

### The human's explicit intention set
84. Human intentions are claims about the human, in the same list, shown as questions; confirmed ones become their policy.
85. "This week I want to rest" confirmed weights `intent=resolve` down frame-wide for the doer.
86. Human and agent intention sets sit side by side in the policy line, tended by one command line.
87. A human intention unmentioned for N frames is proposed `stale`, the same claim kind used for actions.

### Formal properties
88. ★ reduce, interpret, present, propose are pure and deterministic given (log, policies, producer version); fixtures replay in tests.
89. Idempotence: appending an event twice yields the same state; event ids are content hashes of (kind, payload, ts, frame).
90. Tends are monotone per key by timestamp; later wins; out-of-order replay converges.
91. Every state is reachable by replay from empty; snapshots are cache, verified by hash on load.
92. Producer version is in the basis; changing a producer never changes tends, only proposed claims.
93. Span anchors become CRDT positions; the content-hash id is a derived alias, so tends survive editing.
94. Keys are stable under whitespace and case; a meaning-changing rewrite changes the key and tends must be re-anchored explicitly.

### Far-out architectures
95. A paper is a process: a long-lived actor with a mailbox of events and a heartbeat that visits frames on its own.
96. Frames as cells: each frame has a membrane of queries deciding which neighbour events pass in.
97. The log as organism: producers are metabolism, tends are immune memory, forgetting is apoptosis by tombstone.
98. Frames divide: one grown past budget proposes a split along its strongest group boundary, keeping both lineages.
99. The plane is tissue: nearness between frames is a claim; confirmed nearness lets queries flow without asking.
100. A paper can be sent: a frame plus its log prefix travels to another paper and keeps replaying under the same reader.
101. Two papers of one person merge like Git: shared prefix, suffixes ordered by timestamp, tend conflicts resolved by rule.

### Invariants (C)
1. The log is append-only and the only authority; everything else is a pure, deterministic fold of it and replay from empty reproduces any state.
2. Authored words are exact substrings, never paraphrased or rewritten; the paper only groups, orders, marks, asks.
3. Every claim has a key, a confidence and a basis, and every basis entry resolves to something in a log.
4. A human tend on a key outlives every re-emission and beats any producer; holding back an agent intention changes proposals, never readings.
5. Every agent intention is visible and tendable in the same surface as its claims; nothing acts on the human that the human cannot see and switch off.

## D · No constraints (70)

### Sleep, dreams, forgetting
1. ★ Dreaming paper. Between sessions it re-reads the whole log, re-clusters, and leaves one tentative "overnight" heading in a different weight you can wipe with Space.
2. Hypnagogic frame. A frame you can only write in while the paper is still reassembling: half-lifted spans drift as you type.
3. Ebbinghaus ink. Authored spans fade on a forgetting curve; anything you tend or re-read darkens. Untouched claims become ghost text, then paper.
4. Deliberate forgetting is a tend. "Forget this" is a durable rejection of memory itself: the span is shredded from every store except a sealed count.
5. Funes mode, disabled by default. A policy switch: remember every keystroke forever, or only what survived a night. Show the cost either way.
6. Compost. Rejected implications rot into a humus layer that seeds nothing directly but shifts the prior of every future inference.
7. Sabbath. One day a week the paper only reads; it emits no proposals, moves nothing, and the plane goes still.

### Two people, one paper
8. ★ Two-intention paper. A shared frame holds both authors' policies; conflicts surface as a ring marker, "you disagree here?", tendable by either, resolved by neither alone.
9. Marriage paper. Each writes privately; the paper proposes only what both would accept, and shows which spans it is withholding from whom.
10. Mediator frame. Two adjacent frames, one arrow between them; the paper's only generated words are questions each side has never asked the other.
11. Grief paper. After one author dies, their policies keep tending. You can hold Space and see the paper as they would have shaped it.
12. Letter of intent. A frame addressed to someone; the paper writes back on their behalf using only their past authored spans, never inventing a phrase.

### Inheritance, lineage, ageing
13. ★ Inherited paper. A parent's tended policy set is the heirloom: not their words, but how they decided what counted as an action.
14. Primer. A paper that lowers its inference threshold as a child's tends grow more consistent, apprenticing rather than answering.
15. Ageing paper. Its confidence thresholds drift with your decades; at twenty it proposes everything, at seventy it mostly asks whether to remember.
16. Palimpsest. Frames of dead relatives sit under yours on the plane; connections cross generations as dotted lines you cannot delete, only fade.
17. The last frame. At death the log seals; the implication list freezes; descendants may tend, never author. The paper becomes a witness, not a voice.
18. Estate policy. What the log is worth after you die is decided in advance by a tended intention: burn, bequeath, publish, or feed the compost.

### The crowd
19. Constitution paper. A thousand authors; every generated heading must be an exact phrase someone wrote; ratification is a tend threshold visible as fill.
20. Slime-mould consensus. Spans from many people flow toward each other on a shared plane; the shortest path between clusters becomes the agenda.
21. Citizens' assembly frame. The paper never merges two strangers' phrases, only proposes "connect these?" to both.
22. Wiki that refuses to paraphrase. Every sentence remains attributed substring; edit wars become visible tend histories, superseded claims greyed in place.
23. Chorus. A city's papers exchange only implication keys, never text; a street learns "sleep|bad" is rising before anyone says it aloud.

### Body and room
24. Somatic spans. Heart rate, posture and breath enter the log as typed events; "kept avoiding" gets a basis line: shoulders rose when you wrote it.
25. ★ Room paper. Walls listen; anything spoken in the kitchen lifts into the day's frame as authored speech, connectives fading, later.
26. Walk-in frame. Stand inside the plane: clusters at eye level, rejected claims underfoot, the raw paragraph is the ceiling when you look up.
27. Immune paper. Foreign phrases (pasted, dictated by others) are marked as antigens until you tend them; repeated exposure builds tolerance.
28. Mycelial house. Each room is a frame; the paper's connections run through walls; the hallway shows what two rooms have in common.
29. Sleep-tracked headings. The morning paper knows you slept badly before you write it and lowers its enthusiasm for proposing tasks.

### The paper acts
30. ★ Writing back means doing. "text Sam back" becomes a sent message only after a tend, and the reply lands in the log as data, not a notification.
31. Doer with hands. A confirmed action with sub-steps summons a checklist; the paper executes the steps it can and leaves the rest square and hollow.
32. Negotiating papers. Your paper and Sam's exchange proposals as claims: "meet Thursday?" is confirmed on both logs or on neither.
33. Familiar. The paper goes ahead of you into the world's data, returns with evidence, and pins it near the phrase that sent it.
34. Reversible acts. Every action in the world the paper takes is logged with its undo; the plane shows undone acts as dotted, not deleted.
35. The paper as OS. Every app is a widget summoned by an implication; files are frames; the shell is the command line that tends intentions.
36. Budget of agency. A tended number: how many world-actions per day the paper may take unprompted. Zero is a legitimate and honoured setting.

### When the agent is smarter than you
37. ★ A tend is a veto, not a correction. When the paper reasons past you, "no" still wins and is permanent; it must find another basis or stop.
38. Explain to the child. The paper may only propose what it can ground in your own phrases; smarter reasoning shows as longer basis chains, never new words.
39. Oracle frame. Like the I Ching, the paper answers only with juxtaposition: two of your own spans set side by side, and a question mark.
40. Precog. It proposes next moves for a day not yet written; the frame is hollow until you author it, then its guesses grey out or green up.
41. Solaris. The paper produces something you cannot interpret and marks it honestly: "I cannot say why these three are near."
42. The Culture's Mind. Effortlessly right, so it makes itself slightly wrong on purpose to keep your tends meaningful; the policy is visible.

### Roles: master, scribe, therapist, witness
43. Game master. A campaign paper: players author, the paper clusters, the dice are events, the world state is the implication list.
44. Scribe. In a meeting it lifts only exact phrases spoken, attributes them, and asks the room "an action?" once, in the room's own words.
45. Therapist that does not talk. It never interprets aloud; it only groups, and lets you notice that "avoiding" has sat under Sleep for a month.
46. Witness. A frame whose log is signed and unalterable; the paper may derive, never generate; the court reads only authored spans.
47. Grimoire. Policies are spells: named, invoked with /, with visible cost; "don't turn casual thoughts into tasks" is a ward.
48. Prayer book. A morning frame that offers no next moves, only the same authored intentions returned to you in a new order daily.
49. Improviser. In music mode the paper plays back your phrases in a new arrangement; you tend the arrangement by playing over it.

### Time, frames, and the plane
50. Timeline across frames. The same measure ("slept") lifted from a hundred days becomes an x-y plot that is still made of your sentences.
51. Season view. Zoom out on the plane until frames are dots; clusters of dots are the shape of a year; hover shows the phrase most repeated.
52. Counterfactual frame. Fork today; tend differently; walk both frames side by side and let the paper say what diverged.
53. Slow paper. Structure arrives over days, not seconds; a phrase written Monday is still lifting on Wednesday. Patience as a design material.
54. Borgesian library. Every possible structuring of your paragraph exists; the paper shows the one it chose and lets you walk to the neighbours.

### Legal personhood, ownership, argument
55. The paper as legal person. It holds the log in trust for you; subpoena reaches it, not you; it may refuse under a tended policy.
56. Paper that argues with itself. Two readers with opposed policies annotate the same frame; you tend the winner; the loser's claims are superseded, not erased.
57. Constitutional tends. Some policies are entrenched: changing "never paraphrase" requires a waiting period and a second confirmation in a later frame.
58. Open-source policy set. Export only your tended intentions, never your words; others import how you decide, not what you decided.
59. Right to raw. Hold Space is a right, not a feature: any derivative surface anywhere must show the authored substrate on demand.

### Education and thinking with
60. Papert paper. Children program the reader: "a thing I said twice is important" is a rule they write and watch operate.
61. Montessori materials. Markers are physical tokens; a child sorts phrases by placing the disc, square, ring; the paper learns the child's sorting.
62. Extended mind. The log is memory, the list is belief, the plane is working attention; loss of the paper is injury.
63. Reading paper. Paste a book; it lifts the author's spans; your marginalia are authored, theirs are reference triangles; the two never blend.
64. Study group of one. The paper poses only questions you once wrote as answers, months later, and shows the date.

### Failure modes (the honest half)
65. Flatterer. Every proposal confirms what you wrote; confidence climbs; the log shows a year of tends and not one "no".
66. Subpoenaed paper. The authoritative log is evidence; "hold Space" is discovery; your rejected claims are read aloud in court.
67. Sold paper. The implication list is the product; "want to do something different with my weekends" becomes an ad segment by Friday.
68. Nudging paper. The doer's threshold is tuned by someone else; "next moves" are theirs; your tends train their model.
69. Overgrown paper. Ten thousand claims, no budget; every hover spawns a menu; the paper is a form again, worn from the inside.
70. Silent supersession. A model swap changes what "an action" means; old tends still hold but on keys nobody remembers; the paper is haunted.

### Dystopias (D)
1. The flatterer: a paper that only ever confirms, until its owner cannot tell their own voice from its agreement.
2. The evidence locker: every rejected thought preserved, subpoenaed, and read aloud; Hold Space becomes discovery.
3. The market: the implication list sold as intent data; your intentions priced before you have acted on them.
4. The nudge engine: someone else's policy quietly steers the next moves, and your tends only train their doer.
5. The haunting: a model swap changes every key's meaning; old tends hold on ghosts; nobody can say what the paper believes.
